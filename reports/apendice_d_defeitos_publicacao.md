# Defects of the publication chain (items 17-21)

(Part of the record appendix of the manuscript until the submitted version; moved to the deposit when the manuscript was cut to 20 pages. The paper keeps the record of the changes of state, which its numbers depend on, and the summary paragraph that says what these five are.)

17. *The chain-bias equation printed with the value of the previous state.* Up to the previous version the display equation of Section 3.4 was written into the converter rather than derived from the manuscript source, so the text read −1.26 ± 0.75 min and the equation −2.14 ± 0.78; the converter now takes the equation from the same line as the text (an external check).
18. *The figures of the PDF taken from a manual copy.* The figure files the manuscript includes live in `paper/figures/`, and until this version they were a copy made by hand in an earlier state of the chain, while `src/figuras_nota.py` wrote the current ones to `reports/figures/`. The PDF therefore carried figures of the state before the bar corrections together with text of the current state: the panels showed "D10", "1 of 10" and "observed −0.0359 ± 0.0040" — labels that the script computes from the data, so it was the file that was old, not the code. It survived four versions because the text inside a figure is rasterised and no check of the manuscript or of the PDF reads it. `figuras_nota.py` now mirrors the two directories at every run and asserts they are byte-identical, `src/md_para_tex.py` refuses to convert if they differ, and the text drawn inside each figure is written to `reports/figuras_texto.json` and swept by the same checks as the manuscript (`src/confere_nota.py`). Same family as the previous entry: a number that the chain produces correctly, published from a copy that the chain does not write.
19. *The letter of an appendix taken from its position instead of from its name.* This appendix and the previous one were written in the manuscript source in the order E, D, and LaTeX numbers appendices by order of appearance: the first PDF of this version printed the residual-test appendix as D and this record as E. Every cross-reference still resolved to the right content, the compiler reported no undefined reference, and the thirty-seven checks of `src/confere_nota.py` passed — the printed letter was the only thing wrong, and nothing was looking at it. The converter now requires the n-th appendix heading to carry the n-th letter and stops before writing any TeX. Three defects of this version share one shape (items 17, 18 and this one): the display equation written into the converter, the figures taken from a copy made by hand, and the appendix letter assigned by position. In all three the chain computed the right number and the manuscript published something else, through an artefact the chain does not write. Each now has its own check, and since this version every check runs *before* the TeX file exists, so a manuscript that fails one is not produced at all.
20. *Body citations left in the previous state of the chain.* When the per-target shape correction entered the chain (state (F) of the record above), the tables were regenerated from the new records and the prose was not: 9 numerals in the body still quoted the previous values — the coefficient of V527 Dra as −0.0370 where the table said −0.0368, TIC 232634196 as −0.217 where it said −0.218, and so on across 6 targets, together with the reduced χ² of one of them and five aggregates of Section 5.2.1. Every one was a rounding-scale difference, which is exactly why no reader would catch it and why the manuscript would have carried two states at once. This is the same family as the three entries above with the direction reversed: there the chain computed the right number and an artefact of the document published something else; here the chain computed the right number and the text stayed in the version before. The check added for it (`src/citacoes_tabelas.py`) reads every numeral of the body that cites an entry of Tables 3 or 4 — the coefficient and its bar near a target's name, and any quantity the text itself labels (χ²_red, p_gof, p_curv, p_adv) — from the parquet, and the converter refuses to produce a manuscript while one of them disagrees. What it cannot do is invent the association: a numeral that names no target and carries no label is outside its reach, and the aggregates of Section 5.2.1 were brought into the registry instead. Nor does it reach a claim made in words: the same synchronisation found the aggregate reduced χ² described as *above every one of its 2 000 realisations* when it is above all but fifteen of them, and no check looks at a sentence that states a result without writing a number. That is the mechanism of item 12 again — the quantity existed only in a print, so nothing compared it with anything — and it is the reason the percentile now has an entry in the registry rather than a place in the prose alone.
21. *A check that would have deleted what it was meant to protect.* The reference list was swept for entries never cited in the body, so that each could be cited or removed. The first version of that sweep looked for the author's surname followed by the year within a short window of text that contained no full stop — and "et al." contains one, so it declared 20 of the 26 entries orphaned, including every reference the paper actually uses. Had the output been acted on, the sweep would have removed most of the bibliography; the four real orphans were found only after the pattern was fixed. This is the same family as the four entries above, with the consequence reversed: there a guard was missing and a wrong number reached the manuscript, here a guard was present and wrong, and its error would have been destructive rather than silent. A check earns the right to delete only after it has been tested on a case it must pass, and the test of this one is that the entries the body demonstrably cites come back clean.

22. *A check that ran clean over a wrong text.* The citation check of item 20 was written, run, and reported no divergence; the third reading of the manuscript then found two body sentences still carrying dP/dt = −0.0370 ± 0.0033 s yr⁻¹ for V527 Dra where Table 3 says −0.0368 ± 0.0033 — exactly the family the check exists to catch. The cause was in the association, not in the comparison: a numeral was attributed to a target only if the target was named within 260 characters of it, and in those two sentences the last mention of V527 Dra was 276 and 420 characters back, so the numerals named no target and were never compared with anything. A check reports on what it looked at, and its output says nothing about what it skipped; "no divergences" over a text with two of them is the silent form of failure, and it passed one full round of verification. The repair has three parts and all three are in `src/citacoes_tabelas.py`: the backward search for the naming mention now runs to 1 200 characters and prefers a mention that precedes the numeral over one that follows it; a third route was added for the window test, where the text writes z rather than a coefficient; and the character class that recognises a signed numeral was missing the ASCII hyphen that Python's own f-strings produce, so generated blocks compared clean for the wrong reason. Each repair was seen failing before it was trusted — a z = +1.4 and a −0.0370 planted in the body, both flagged, then removed. The general lesson is the one the paper makes about detections: a guard that has never been shown the case it must catch is an assertion, not a check, and a clean report from it measures its reach and not the text.

23. *A reference edited in the product and restored by the generator.* When the manuscript was cut to 20 pages, the appendix holding the recovery pass moved to the deposit and the remaining appendices were relabelled, so the two rows of Table 2 that pointed to it were edited to point at the deposit instead. The edit was made in the manuscript; those rows are generated by `src/tabela_nota.py`, which still held the old text, and the next regeneration put it back — now pointing at an appendix that exists and is about something else, the control target. Nothing in the document looked wrong: the reference resolved, the table compiled, and the reader would have been sent to the wrong page. This is item 18's mechanism with the roles exchanged — there the document was correct and a hand-made copy of the figures was published, here the source was stale and the document was corrected by hand — and the rule behind both is that a product must never be the place a correction is written. Two guards were added for it in `src/confere_nota.py`, both enforced by the converter before any TeX is written: every generated block of the manuscript is compared with what the generator writes to `reports/tabelas_nota.md`, and every "Appendix X" mentioned in the text must have an "Appendix X" heading.

24. *A set that existed only as a list typed into a patch.* The eight targets of the mean-and-difference test were never selected by code. The count was pre-registered in the expectation, but the membership lived in a literal list inside the patch that wrote the text, and the sentence presenting it — "of the eight targets the window test classifies" — described a different set: the window test classifies seven and holds out V527 Dra, while the list replaces V527 Dra with TIC 229914020 and omits TIC 198388252, which is of the same description in every respect the set could depend on. The defect is not the membership but the absence of a rule: with no rule in code, nothing could compare the set with anything, which is item 20's mechanism applied to a set instead of a number. It was found while registering the block's quantities, hence after the measurement and with the omitted target's value already read, so the set is left as declared and the rule-defined variant is published beside it (Appendix D of the paper). Both are now computed in `src/numeros_da_nota.py`, the list with the reason written next to it.

25. *The guard that found its own defect.* The first of the two guards of item 23 compares every generated block of the manuscript with what the generator writes. On its first run it covered four blocks while the generator injects five: the offset scan of the appendix was assembled only at insertion time and never written to the compared file, so an edit inside that block would have passed the check and been reverted in silence by the next insertion — item 23 exactly, inside the guard for item 23. A guard is a claim about coverage, and the claim has to be checked against what the producer emits, not against what the guard's author remembers it emitting. Fixed by writing that block to the compared file as well, and seen failing with a cell altered inside it.
